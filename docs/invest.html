Cash machines: free cash flow against market value

Companies whose free cash flow, after stock-based pay, is at least 10% of their enterprise value (market value plus net debt: what buying the whole company costs, its debts included), from all US-listed stocks. Measured against market value alone, an indebted company's cash flow looks far bigger than what shareholders get, since lenders are paid first. Stock handed to staff is subtracted too: it costs owners through dilution even though no cash leaves the company. ZIM in 2021 was the extreme case: a year of cash flow close to the price of the entire company. It was also the top of a shipping boom, and its cash flow collapsed in 2023. So the verdict asks whether the cash is durable (high in every one of the last four years) or a peak (one year far above the rest). "4 yrs of FCF vs price" above 1.0× means the last four years of cash flow exceed what the market charges for the whole company today.
